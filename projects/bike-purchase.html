Excel Data Analytics Project

Customer Purchasing Behaviour & Demographics Analysis

An Excel-based analysis exploring customer purchasing behaviour across income, commute distance, age, region and occupation, culminating in an interactive dashboard.

Microsoft Excel Data Cleaning Conditional Logic Pivot Tables Slicers Dashboard
1,026 Customer records in source dataset
4 Workbook analysis sheets
44.3% Purchase rate for the Middle Age group

Project Overview

I worked with a customer purchasing dataset containing demographic, income, commute, occupation, regional and bicycle purchase information.

The project followed an Excel analytics workflow from data preparation and calculated fields through Pivot Table analysis, visualization and dashboard creation.

The main objective was to explore patterns associated with bicycle purchasing behaviour and identify customer segments that showed different purchasing patterns.

Workbook Structure

The project workbook is organised into four main sheets:

  • bike_buyers — original customer dataset
  • Working Sheet — prepared data and analysis fields
  • Pivot Table — summary analysis
  • Dashboard — final visual presentation

Data Preparation

I cleaned and prepared the customer data in Excel before creating the analytical summaries.

I used conditional logic to create an Age Bracket field, grouping customers into Adolescent, Middle Age and Old.

The prepared data was then used to create Pivot Tables and dashboard visualizations.

Key Findings

Income and Purchase Behaviour

Female customers who purchased a bike had an average income of approximately $65,490, compared with $60,274 among female non-buyers.

Male buyers had an average income of approximately $62,679, compared with $62,308 among male non-buyers.

The difference between buyers and non-buyers was therefore more noticeable among female customers than male customers in this analysis.

Commute Distance

Purchase behaviour varied across commute-distance groups. Among customers commuting more than 10 miles, only 3 purchased a bike compared with 37 who did not.

The 0–1 mile group had 30 purchases and 47 non-purchases, while the 2–5 mile group had 34 purchases and 25 non-purchases.

This suggests that commute distance was associated with different purchasing patterns in the analysed customer records.

Age Demographics

The Middle Age group had the highest purchase rate at 44.3%, with 89 buyers and 112 non-buyers.

The Adolescent group had a purchase rate of 20.8%, while the Old group had a purchase rate of 18.1%.

Regional Analysis

I used Pivot Tables and dashboard visualizations to explore purchasing behaviour across geographic regions and compare customer purchase patterns between regions.

Occupational Analysis

I also examined purchasing behaviour across occupational categories to identify differences in the number of buyers and non-buyers across professional groups.

Dashboard

The final Excel dashboard brings the analysis together through visual summaries of customer purchasing behaviour.

The dashboard includes visual analysis of:

  • Average income by purchased bike status
  • Customer commute distance
  • Customer age brackets
  • Customer purchasing behaviour

Pivot Tables and slicers were used to make the analysis easier to explore and compare across customer segments.

Limitations

The analysis is based on the customer records contained in the provided workbook and describes patterns within this dataset.

The findings show associations between customer characteristics and bicycle purchasing behaviour. They do not establish that income, commute distance, age, region or occupation directly causes a customer to purchase a bicycle.

Some Pivot Table analyses use the records available in their respective analytical summaries, so individual analysis totals should not automatically be interpreted as representing every record in the original dataset.

Conclusion

This project allowed me to practise using Excel as a complete data analysis tool, from data preparation and calculated fields through Pivot Table analysis and dashboard creation.

It helped me develop practical experience in customer segmentation, exploratory analysis, data visualization and communicating findings through an interactive Excel dashboard.

It also gave me experience turning a customer dataset into a structured analysis that could be used to explore purchasing patterns and potential customer segments.
