Project Overview

Healthcare data does not exist in isolation. Every patient record, laboratory result, vaccination register and disease notification is produced within a healthcare system shaped by governance, funding, workforce and service delivery.

This project compares the healthcare systems of South Africa, Lesotho and the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) to understand how differences in healthcare-system design influence health information, reporting and decision-making.

The project also explores practical applications of healthcare analytics in Southern Africa, including disease surveillance, healthcare KPIs, reporting quality, resource allocation and evidence-based planning.

Project Objectives

  • Compare healthcare systems across South Africa, Lesotho and the DRC.
  • Understand how healthcare structures influence data collection and reporting.
  • Identify strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats within each system.
  • Explore practical applications of healthcare analytics in Southern Africa.
  • Recommend data-driven strategies that could support improved healthcare delivery.
  • Build foundational knowledge for future healthcare analytics projects using DHIS2, SQL, Python, Excel and Tableau.

Healthcare Systems Compared

🇿🇦 South Africa

The analysis examined South Africa's public and private healthcare sectors, governance through national, provincial and district structures, levels of care, community health worker programmes, healthcare financing and major health challenges.

The research also considered National Health Insurance and the unequal distribution of healthcare resources between public and private sectors.

🇱🇸 Lesotho

The analysis examined the role of the Ministry of Health, the country's district-based healthcare structure, collaboration between government and faith-based providers and healthcare delivery across different levels of care.

Particular attention was given to Village Health Workers and the challenges of providing healthcare across mountainous and remote communities.

🇨🇩 Democratic Republic of the Congo

The analysis examined the DRC's decentralized healthcare structure, Provincial Health Divisions, Health Zones, community-level healthcare delivery and the role of General Referral Hospitals.

The research also considered the challenges created by resource constraints, fragmented healthcare provision and disease outbreaks.

Healthcare Analytics Perspective

A major focus of the project was understanding how healthcare analytics can support decision-making within different healthcare systems.

The project explored how routine healthcare data can support:

  • Disease surveillance
  • Healthcare KPI monitoring
  • Resource allocation
  • Reporting quality
  • Service delivery monitoring
  • Healthcare planning
  • Evidence-based policy decisions

DHIS2 & Disease Surveillance in the DRC

The project explored how DHIS2 could support disease surveillance and health information management in the DRC.

Potential applications include:

  • Improving routine disease reporting
  • Monitoring disease trends
  • Supporting outbreak detection
  • Identifying geographic patterns
  • Improving reporting completeness
  • Providing decision-makers with more timely information

The analysis also highlighted the importance of strengthening the quality and timeliness of data rather than focusing only on data collection.

Healthcare Analytics Opportunities in Lesotho

The project explored practical opportunities for using healthcare analytics to strengthen the Lesotho health system.

  • Monitoring healthcare service delivery
  • Improving reporting completeness
  • Supporting community health worker programmes
  • Identifying geographic gaps in healthcare access
  • Monitoring maternal and child health indicators
  • Tracking medicine availability
  • Using dashboards to support district-level decisions

The analysis highlighted the importance of designing digital tools that reflect the realities of rural and mountainous communities.

KPIs for South Africa

The project explored potential healthcare indicators that could support monitoring of healthcare-system performance under National Health Insurance.

  • CCMDD penetration rate
  • Facility waiting times
  • Primary-care referral leakage
  • Healthcare service utilization
  • Reporting completeness
  • Medicine availability

These indicators could help identify operational problems and support evidence-based healthcare decision-making.

SWOT Analysis

South Africa

The SWOT analysis considered the country's specialized tertiary healthcare infrastructure, national laboratory network, digital-health opportunities and National Health Insurance alongside public-sector pressure and healthcare inequalities.

Lesotho

The analysis considered the strength of the community health worker network and community-level healthcare access alongside geographic barriers, limited resources and opportunities for digital health and improved reporting.

Democratic Republic of the Congo

The analysis considered the decentralized Health Zone structure and community-level organization alongside resource constraints, fragmented healthcare provision, data challenges and opportunities for DHIS2 and digital disease surveillance.

Recommendations

Improve DHIS2 Reporting Completeness

Reporting-completeness dashboards could help identify facilities with missing or delayed reports.

Strengthen Community Health Worker Support

Offline-capable digital tools could reduce the burden of paper-based registers and improve the speed of data transmission for community health workers in rural areas.

Improve Supply-Chain Monitoring

Supply-chain dashboards could help identify potential medicine and vaccine stock-outs.

Increase Interoperability

Stronger connections between separate information systems could improve the sharing of relevant information between healthcare providers and public health structures.

Expand Digital Health Infrastructure

Rural digital-health initiatives need to consider practical requirements such as reliable electricity, device charging and connectivity.

What I Learned

This project changed how I understand healthcare analytics.

I learned that meaningful healthcare analysis also requires an understanding of the healthcare system that produces the data.

Comparing South Africa, Lesotho and the DRC showed me how governance, geography, community participation, funding and system design can influence healthcare delivery and the information generated by those systems.

Most importantly, I learned that healthcare data represents real people and real experiences. A missing report may reflect staffing shortages. Low vaccination coverage may reflect transport barriers. A healthcare indicator is therefore not simply a number; it can represent a real experience within a community.

Limitations

This is a comparative healthcare-systems research project rather than a quantitative analysis of national healthcare datasets.

The findings are based on the sources reviewed for the report and should therefore be interpreted within the scope of those sources.

Healthcare systems are complex and continue to change, particularly in areas such as digital health, National Health Insurance, disease surveillance and healthcare financing.

The project provides a high-level comparison of three countries and cannot capture every regional, provincial, district or facility-level difference within each system.

The recommendations are practical analytics and digital-health opportunities identified through the research. They are not presented as formal government policy proposals.

Conclusion

This project helped me understand that healthcare analytics begins before the data reaches a spreadsheet, database or dashboard.

The structure of a healthcare system influences how data is collected, who reports it, how complete it is and how confidently it can be used for decision-making.

Comparing South Africa, Lesotho and the Democratic Republic of the Congo helped me develop a stronger understanding of the relationship between healthcare systems and health information.

It also helped me identify practical ways that healthcare analytics, DHIS2, digital tools and better reporting could support healthcare decision-making in resource-constrained settings.

As I continue developing my technical skills in Python, SQL, Excel, Tableau and DHIS2, I want to combine those skills with a deeper understanding of Southern African healthcare systems.
