Healthcare Analytics Project

Healthcare Revenue Cycle Management Analytics

An end-to-end analysis of healthcare claims to explore reimbursement efficiency, payer performance, claim denials and potential revenue leakage.

Microsoft Excel PostgreSQL Python Pandas Tableau

Project Overview

I worked with a healthcare Revenue Cycle Management dataset containing hospital claim information. The project followed an end-to-end analytics workflow, starting with data preparation and continuing through SQL analysis, Python analysis and Tableau visualization.

The goal was to understand how effectively billed healthcare services were converted into payments and to identify areas where claims were being denied or revenue was not being collected.

What I Found

Overall collection performance The analysis produced an overall collection rate of 89.98%, calculated from $200,754 in payments received against $223,112 in allowed amounts.
Billed-to-paid gap The difference between total billed charges and total payments was $96,437. This represents the billed-to-paid gap and includes both amounts not collected and the difference between billed and allowed amounts.
Payer performance Medicaid produced the highest net collection rate at 90.43%, while Self-Pay had the lowest at 89.48%. Commercial was 90.18% and Medicare was 89.77%.
Primary denial driver Incorrect Billing Information was the largest denial category, representing $16,740 in financial exposure across 52 denied claims.
Other major denial drivers Lack of Medical Necessity accounted for $12,157 across 38 claims, while Service Not Covered accounted for $12,093 across 43 claims.

What I Learned

I also gained practical experience working with healthcare claims concepts such as payer types, claim status, denial reasons, reimbursement and accounts receivable.
